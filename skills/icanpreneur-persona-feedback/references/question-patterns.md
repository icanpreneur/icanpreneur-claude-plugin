# Question patterns

Put the material in the question. Keep the user's text verbatim. Ask for reasoning, not verdicts. Replace the brackets.

## Offer or proposal

> Here is an offer we are considering: [offer text or URL]. How do you react to it? What appeals to you, what makes you hesitate, and what would you need to know before buying?

Follow-ups:

- "What is the main reason you would say no?"
- "Who else would need to agree before you bought this, and what would they ask?"
- "What would you compare this to today?"

## Price or packaging

> We are thinking of offering [plans and prices]. Tell me how you would react to each. What would you expect to be included, which would you choose, and what would make the price feel too high or too low?

Ask about expectations and trade-offs. Do not ask the persona to name an exact price they would pay, or what share of people would buy.

## Headline, message or positioning statement

> Here are two ways to describe what we do. A: [text]. B: [text]. What does each tell you? What is unclear or hard to believe in each? Which would make you keep reading, and why?

Use neutral labels. If the choice matters, repeat in a new conversation with the order swapped.

## Landing-page or email copy

> Here is the copy: [text or URL]. Walk me through what you notice first, what you understand, what you doubt, and what you would do next.

## Sales pitch or call script

> Here is the pitch we would use: [text]. Where would you stop listening? What objections come to mind, and what proof would you want to see?

## Product idea or feature

> We are considering adding [feature]. Does it connect to a problem you actually have? How do you handle that today? What would make you use it, and what would make you ignore it?

## Client deliverable (for consultants)

> This is a draft [deliverable] for [client's] customers: [text]. How would a person like you read it? What is confusing, missing or off-putting?

Use the persona that matches the client's segment. Make clear to the client afterwards that the reaction is simulated.

## Follow-ups that go deeper

- "Explain your biggest concern in your own words, and what would settle it."
- "What would you need to see in a demo?"
- "What would make you trust this claim?"
- "What would you tell a colleague about this?"

## Avoid

- Leading questions: "Isn't this clearer?"
- Asking for numbers, forecasts or market sizes.
- Several unrelated questions at once. One topic per question.
- Asking the persona to agree with a decision already made.
- Telling the persona who it is. The service already knows.
