# Icanpreneur definitions

These definitions are used across the platform, including by the checks behind `validate_canvas_item`. Use them when you create, judge, rewrite or discuss Canvas items, scripts and positioning. If a user's wording is looser, help them tighten it instead of accepting it.

The Canvas has nine boxes: `CustomerSegments`, `Problem`, `Solution`, `UniqueValueProposition`, `UnfairAdvantage`, `Channel`, `KeyMetrics`, `CostStructure`, `RevenueStreams`. Platform definitions are available here for the first five and for traction. For `Channel`, `KeyMetrics`, `CostStructure` and `RevenueStreams` there is no definition in this skill. Do not invent criteria for them. Call `validate_canvas_item` if the user wants a check, and say that it uses credits.

## Customer Segment (`CustomerSegments`)

A group of customers similar enough that:

1. They need the same **Job-to-be-Done** and share needs, wants, behaviors or other attributes.
2. The same product or service appeals to all of them.
3. The same message and marketing channels can reach them.
4. Word of mouth exists between them, so they can be strong references for each other.

Also check that the segment is likely to care about the problem and willing to pay for a solution, and is defined by more than a job title or role. How to apply it: "small business owners" or "millennials" usually fails tests 1 and 3. When a user gives a broad group, ask what job they are trying to get done and how you would reach them, then propose narrower candidates. Do not create several segments from one vague sentence. One research project belongs to one segment.

## Job-to-be-Done

The progress a customer wants to make in a given situation. People "hire" products to get a job done. A job can be small ("pass the time in a queue") or significant ("find a more fulfilling career"). Describe jobs in terms of the situation and the progress wanted, not the product.

## Customer Problem (`Problem`)

An obstacle between customers and what they need, want or value. A good one to focus on:

- is something enough people care about and are ready to pay to solve, and
- is not adequately addressed by any existing solution.

Also check that problems are linked to one or more segments, distinct from each other, and clear enough to validate through research. How to apply it: ask what customers do today and what is wrong with it. A problem with no stated alternative is unproven.

## Unique Value Proposition (`UniqueValueProposition`)

One clear, compelling message about why the offer is different and worth buying over any alternative. It is:

- **Unique and different**: it states value that current alternatives either cannot or do not offer.
- **Valuable**: the target segment values it enough to pay the asking price.
- **Clear**: short enough to get a target customer's attention in 5 to 10 seconds.

A UVP comes from the Customer Segments and their inadequately solved Customer Problems. It lives in the deficiencies and friction points of the alternatives. When drafting or judging one, name the segment, the problem and the alternative it beats.

## Solution (`Solution`)

A product or service the segment will pay for to solve their problems. A good solution addresses all the important problems and delivers on the UVP.

## Unfair Advantage (`UnfairAdvantage`)

The hard-to-replicate "secret sauce". It has two aspects:

1. **Unfair outcomes**: what makes what you are building hard to replicate.
2. **Unfair capabilities**: hard-to-replicate internal capabilities that let you win customers over anyone else.

A strong one is **unique** (hard to copy or acquire), **valuable** (tied to something customers value highly) and **growing** (stronger over time relative to competitors). Being first, having a good idea or working hard are not unfair advantages.

## Traction

Quantitative evidence of customer demand: the level of interest and engagement a product generates, such as a high rate of customer acquisition or, for a free product, a growing user base. Interviews, personas and simulated results are not traction.

## Traction channels

Canvas `Channel` items are ordinary Canvas items. Read, create and edit them like the other boxes. What is unavailable is traction-channel **strategy** generation: there is no tool and no document type for it. Do not offer to generate one. If the user asks for traction-channel strategy, discuss it in general terms without Icanpreneur tools.
